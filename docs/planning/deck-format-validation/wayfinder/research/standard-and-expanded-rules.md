# Research: Standard and Expanded construction rules (as of 2026-10-10)

**Label:** wayfinder:research

## Question

What are the current Standard and Expanded construction rules for the Pokémon TCG? This covers the legal regulation marks and the rotation schedule, the Expanded cutoff, the ban lists, the per-deck limits, the rule for older printings and reprints, and how often each of these changes.

## Findings

### 1. Standard: legal regulation marks and rotation

- **Legal marks: H, I, J and any later mark.** G rotated out. P1 says: "Cards with the 'G' regulation mark will no longer be legal for play in the 2026 Standard format." Cards marked H, I and J are legal, "as will any future regulation marks that may be released". *Confidence: high (primary).*
- **Legality follows the mark, not the set.** P1: "A card's legality status is not based on which expansion it's from." *High.*
- **Effective dates of the current rotation (P1):**
  - Announced 9 Jan 2026.
  - Pokémon TCG Live: 26 Mar 2026.
  - In-person Play! Pokémon events: 10 Apr 2026.
  - *High.*
- **The next rotation has not been announced** as of 2026-10-10. No pokemon.com announcement was found, and the latest announcement linked from P6 is Pitch Black (P5), which does not mention rotation.
  - Past pattern (inference, not a schedule): the 2025 and 2026 rotations were both announced on 9 January and took effect in April.
  - The next rotation would presumably remove H, the oldest legal mark. That is an inference only.
  - The 2009–10 and 2022–23 seasons had no rotation (S1). Rotation is a yearly custom, not a guarantee.
  - *Confidence that nothing has been announced: medium. Absence of evidence comes from search plus P6.*
- **Sets that fully rotated in 2026 (S1, secondary):** Scarlet & Violet base, Paldea Evolved, Obsidian Flames, 151, Paradox Rift and Paldean Fates, plus McDonald's Collection 2023. Cards from these sets, and from later sets, are legal only where the individual printing carries H or later.
- **New-card waiting period.** The sources give three different descriptions:
  - P1: "typically two weeks after their release".
  - P7 (Nov 2025): "the second Friday following that product or expansion's US market release date".
  - S1, citing a @playpokemon post of 28 Jan 2026: two weeks after the earliest release of the Sleeved Booster, Booster Display or Booster Bundle; for special sets, two weeks after the earliest release of the Booster Bundle or Elite Trainer Box.
  - Promos (P8): "legal on the first or third Friday of the month after the product is released". P8 lists the legal SVP and MEP promos, plus four SWSH promos that are still legal through reprint (Poké Ball SWSH146; Professor's Research SWSH152 and SWSH178; Boss's Orders SWSH251). P7 says P8 is updated monthly.
  - A new printing of an already-legal card is legal **immediately on release** (P7). S1, citing a @playpokemon post of 21 May 2026, says reprints obtained at a Prerelease may be used as soon as they are obtained. Search snippets show that the 21 May 2026 handbook revision added this Prerelease-reprint clarification.
- **First legal date of the J mark: unverified.** Search snippets of the May 2026 handbook table show a "March 6, 2026 | J" row, but that extraction was garbled.

### 2. Expanded card pool cutoff

P1 describes Expanded as unchanged and "maintaining the Black & White Series and forward". P7's legal-expansions tables (Nov 2025 revision) list the pool in detail:

- **Every regulation-marked card:** D, E, F, and all marks that are legal in Standard.
- **Black & White sets:** Black & White, Emerging Powers, Noble Victories, Next Destinies, Dark Explorers, Dragon Vault, Dragons Exalted, Boundaries Crossed, Plasma Storm, Plasma Freeze. Plasma Blast and Legendary Treasures are missing from the mirror's table. This looks like a mirror transcription gap, and the official table should be checked.
- **XY sets:** Kalos Starter Set through Evolutions, including Double Crisis and Generations.
- **Sun & Moon sets:** Sun & Moon through Cosmic Eclipse, including Shining Legends, Dragon Majesty and Hidden Fates.
- **Additional products:**
  - McDonald's Collection 2011–2021.
  - The BW and XY Trainer Kits, and the two SM Trainer Kits.
  - Detective Pikachu.
- **Black Star promos:** BW 01 and up, XY 01 and up, SM 158 and up. SM promos 1–157 lack an explicit marker and are not listed. *Medium confidence on this detail. Check it against the current PDF.*

The handbook's reprint rule (section 4 below) covers both Standard and Expanded. So, read literally, a pre-BW printing (for example a HeartGold & SoulSilver-era Trainer) is playable in Expanded if a functionally identical printing with the same name is Expanded-legal.

*Confidence: high for "BW-onward plus marks D and later"; medium for exact list membership, because it was read from an older revision on a mirror.*

### 4. Older printings and reprints: what "equivalent" means

**Primary rule.** P7, section "Reprinted Cards in the Standard & Expanded Formats" (Nov 2025 text; search snippets show it retained in May 2026):

> the older version of the card may be played if:
> - The name of the new card is identical to that of the older card;
> - All text printed on the new card is functionally identical to that of the older card.

The same section also says that if a card meets those criteria, "a newer version of a card that is currently playable in the Standard or Expanded format may be played immediately in that format upon its release."

**Meaning of "functionally identical" (P7 examples):**

- Copycat (CES 127) and Copycat (TRR 83) are functionally identical: the wording changed, but the effect did not.
- Rainbow Energy (CES 151) and Rainbow Energy (TR 17) are not. One "put[s] 1 damage counter" and the other "does 10 damage", and damage counters and damage are separate mechanics.
- "Text" is broader than attacks and abilities: it covers all printed text. For Pokémon this presumably includes HP, type, weakness, resistance, retreat cost and stage. That reading is an inference, since P7 gives only Trainer and Energy examples.

**Standard-specific statement (P1):** "Older versions of cards that don't have a regulation mark can still be used if the card is currently legal for play." P1 gives two examples, and both are Trainers:

- Rare Candy from Sun & Moon (no mark) is playable because the Mega Evolution Rare Candy carries I.
- Boss's Orders from Rebel Clash carries D, which has rotated, and is still playable because the Mega Evolution printing carries I.

So the rule extends to marked cards whose own mark has rotated, not only to unmarked ones. P1 sends readers to the handbook "for further clarification on which reprints are OK to use."

**Unlimited, for contrast (P7):** older Trainer versions with the same name but different effects may be played, using the wording of the most recent version.

**Errata.** A TCG Errata PDF exists ([tcg_errata.pdf](https://www.pokemon.com/static-assets/content-assets/cms2/pdf/trading-card-game/tcg_errata.pdf), linked from P6; not read). S1 (secondary) says that older prints whose wording differs significantly cannot be used unless they have received official errata. Errata can therefore change whether two printings count as functionally identical.

**OPEN CONFLICT: Pokémon versus Trainer substitution.**

- Bulbapedia (S1, secondary, no citation visible) says: "If a Trainer card has a print with a legal regulation mark, any print of that Trainer card is allowed to be used in its place … However, a Pokémon card with a rotated regulation mark cannot be used in place of a functionally identical Pokémon card with a legal regulation mark."
- The primary reprint rule (P7) makes no Pokémon/Trainer distinction.
- P1's examples are all Trainers.
- A targeted search found no primary clarification.
- *Confidence in either reading: low.* This is the central reprint-equivalence question for the validator, and it remains unresolved until a primary source (the current handbook text, a @playpokemon ruling or a rules Q&A) settles it.

**Card identity for the 4-copy count.** P7 counts copies "as defined by the card's English language translation", so the count is by name across all printings: 2 Sun & Moon Rare Candy plus 2 Mega Evolution Rare Candy reach the cap of 4. Naming edge cases:

- Subtitled reprints are listed with parenthetical subtitles, for example "Professor's Research (Professor Juniper)" and "Boss's Orders (Cyrus)" in P8. That these count under the base name is widely understood but was **not confirmed from primary text this session** (secondary; medium).
- Owner-prefixed names ("Team Rocket's Mewtwo ex" and "Mewtwo ex") and suffixes such as ex, V, VSTAR, GX and EX are different names. *Secondary; medium-high.*

### 5. Deck construction and per-deck limits

**Constructed rules (P7, Nov 2025 text; snippets confirm retained in May 2026):**

- "The deck must contain exactly 60 cards."
- "Decks may not contain more than 4 copies of a single card, as defined by the card's English language translation, except for Basic Energy cards."
- Matches are played for 6 Prize cards.
- *High.*

**Basic Energy:**

- Unlimited copies (P7).
- Basic Energy carries no regulation mark and is always legal (S1, secondary; high).

**Special per-deck limits.** These are printed as rule text on the cards themselves (P9, not verified this session) and described in S2. The three limits are shaped differently:

- **ACE SPEC:** no more than **1 ACE SPEC card in the deck, in total.** The ACE SPECs from the BW era (Boundaries Crossed through Plasma Blast, Expanded-only) and from the SV/Mega Evolution era share one cap in Expanded. That rests on the shared "ACE SPEC" rule wording, which was not verified this session. *Medium-high.*
- **Radiant Pokémon:** no more than **1 Radiant Pokémon in the deck, in total.** Radiant cards are from Sword & Shield and are Expanded-only now. *Medium-high.*
- **Prism Star (◇):** no more than **1 Prism Star card with the same name**, so different Prism Star cards may coexist. They are from Sun & Moon and Expanded-only. *Medium-high.*

Other once-per-game mechanics (GX attacks, VSTAR Powers) are gameplay rules, not construction rules.

**Physical-card legality (P7), relevant only for physical decks:**

- Japanese cards count as "marked" because their backs differ, so they are not legal at sanctioned events.
- Legal languages depend on the rating zone.
- Mixed-language decks are allowed if the card backs match.

### 6. How and how often each rule changes, and where to check

| What | How it changes | Cadence | Where to check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Legal marks / rotation | Rotation announcement | About yearly, announced in January and effective in April (2025 and 2026), with occasional skipped years | pokemon.com news (P1-style announcement); P7 legal-expansions table |
| New sets entering Standard and Expanded | Automatic once the waiting period ends | About 6 expansions a year (P7) | P7 release schedule; per-set announcements |
| Promo legality | Promo legality page | Updated monthly (P7) | P8 |
| Ban lists | Per-set "Banned List and Rule Changes" announcement | Roughly every expansion; announcement about 4 weeks before the effective date | P2 (canonical list); announcements linked from P6 |
| Handbook (deck rules, reprint rule, Expanded table) | Revised document with a "Summary of Changes" | "Reviewed on a quarterly basis" (P7). Revisions seen: Aug 2025, Nov 2025, Apr 2026, May 2026 | P7 |
| Errata | Errata PDF | Irregular | tcg_errata.pdf (P6) |

**Observed fact for "where rules data lives":** none of these are machine-readable feeds. They are HTML pages and PDFs. In this session, pokemon.com served bot-challenge pages to non-browser HTTP clients for the PDFs, the banned-list page (on some attempts) and the announcements. Other sites mirror the content, but those mirrors are secondary. The pokemon.com card database has a "Standard format-legal" filter (linked from P8), but this session did not check whether it offers a usable API.

## Implications for the validator (facts only)

- Standard legality of a printing depends on its mark (H or later), and also on whether **any** printing with the same name and functionally identical text carries a legal mark. That requires grouping printings into equivalence classes by name and text. Whether Pokémon printings may substitute is unresolved (see the open conflict in section 4).
- Expanded legality depends on set membership (BW onward, the listed products and promos) or on a D-or-later mark, plus the same reprint rule, minus the per-printing ban list.
- Limit checks need per-printing flags for ACE SPEC, Radiant and Prism Star, plus whether the card is a Basic Energy. The 4-copy count groups by English name.
- Every rules input changes over time: marks once a year, bans and set additions every few months, the handbook every quarter. A hard-coded snapshot needs a date and a refresh path.

## Open uncertainties

1. Pokémon versus Trainer reprint substitution across rotated marks: the primary text and Bulbapedia disagree. *Low confidence either way.*
2. The current (21 May 2026) handbook text could not be read in full. The Standard and Expanded tables, J's legality date, and any wording changes since Nov 2025 are unverified beyond search snippets.
3. Exact Expanded set list: Plasma Blast and Legendary Treasures are missing from the mirror's table, and the SM promo range starts at SM158.
4. The per-deck limit wording for ACE SPEC, Radiant and Prism Star was not verified against card scans or the Rulebook this session.
5. Whether subtitled reprints (Professor's Research, Boss's Orders) share a name for the 4-copy count: widely understood, not primary-verified here.
6. No announcement of the next rotation was found. Its timing is inferred from past years.
