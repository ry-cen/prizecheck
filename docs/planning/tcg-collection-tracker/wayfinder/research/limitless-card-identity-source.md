# Limitless TCG as a Card Identity source

Research checked 2026-09-28 (America/Los_Angeles). Scope: the public Limitless card catalog, its published developer documentation, and its relevance to the English-only Pokémon TCG collection MVP. “Card Identity” here means the rules-relevant *same printed name* group for the four-copy deck limit; it is not one mechanical card design or one physical Printing. The [official Pokémon rules manual](https://www.pokemon-card.com/assets/document/manual.pdf) says cards with the same name count together even when their attacks or illustrations differ. The [prior cross-language source audit](cross-language-card-identity-source.md) covers that rule and translation candidates in more detail.

## Bottom line

**Do not use Limitless “other printings” as Card Identity, and do not plan automated ingestion from its human-facing pages as an MVP dependency.** Its grouped printings are useful evidence for reprints, alternate arts, and some English↔Japanese *same-card* links, but they are narrower than the same-name group required by the copy limit. Limitless documents a tournament-platform API, not a card-catalog API or downloadable identity feed. For the English-only MVP, TCGdex's required `name` field on each Printing already supplies the data needed to construct application-owned, exact-normalized-name groups, with explicit handling for rule exceptions and source errors. ([TCGdex Card schema](https://tcgdex.dev/reference/card); [Limitless search syntax](https://limitlesstcg.com/cards/syntax); [Limitless developer guide](https://docs.limitlesstcg.com/developer.html))

## What “other printings” means in practice

Limitless describes `unique:prints` as all matching prints, `unique:cards` as one print per unique card with reprints/different arts ignored, and `unique:text` as one result per unique card-and-language combination. That distinguishes its *card* grouping from individual *prints*, but it does not assert that “card” means every different card with the same printed name. ([Search syntax, “Duplicate Cards”](https://limitlesstcg.com/cards/syntax))

The concrete counterexample is two currently published `Pikachu ex` pages:

| Limitless page | Card text | Listed international printings | Listed Japanese printings |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Surging Sparks #57](https://limitlesstcg.com/cards/SSP/57) | 200 HP, Resolute Heart, Topaz Bolt | SSP #57/#219/#238/#247; Prismatic Evolutions #179; Ascended Heroes #57/#277 | Super Electric Breaker #33/#122/#132/#136 and later reprints |
| [30th Celebration #54](https://limitlesstcg.com/cards/30C/54) | 190 HP, Zip-Zap Frenzy, Thunder | 30C #54/#150 | Mega Promotional Cards #132; 30th Celebration #48 |

Both have the exact displayed English name `Pikachu ex`, yet each page's printing list excludes the other card. Under the name-based four-copy rule, they belong to the **same Card Identity**. The observed Limitless grouping instead follows a narrower same-card/reprint relationship. This is an observation from these pages, not a claim about Limitless's private matching algorithm. The [Japanese SV8 #33 page](https://limitlesstcg.com/cards/jp/SV8/33) does link to SSP #57 and displays `ピカチュウex`, illustrating where the relation *can* supply a candidate cross-language printing/name pair; it still cannot enumerate every same-name card.

The page lists set and collector number per linked printing. It does not expose separate normal/reverse/holo finish rows for the same set-number on these inspected pages. By contrast, [TCGdex documents](https://tcgdex.dev/reference/card) `variants.normal`, `variants.reverse`, `variants.holo`, and `variants.firstEdition` flags on its Card object. Limitless's printing lists may help spot art or set-level reprints absent in TCGdex, but they are not a complete physical-edition inventory model. This conclusion is limited to the inspected page representation; it is not a measured catalog-wide completeness claim.

## Coverage, IDs, provenance, and updates

- Limitless says its card database covers **all Standard and Expanded legal cards** in seven languages. It does not claim a complete collector catalog of every historical physical printing. That matters for a collection app whose inventory is not restricted to active formats. ([About](https://limitlesstcg.com/about))
- Its public card URLs identify rows by set code and number, e.g. `/cards/SSP/57` and `/cards/jp/SV8/33`. These are useful human references, but the inspected documentation does not promise a stable machine ID or immutable cross-language identity key. The public pages do not show a per-card revision timestamp or machine change feed. Treat an apparent URL convention as an observation, not a contractual API. ([English example](https://limitlesstcg.com/cards/SSP/57); [Japanese example](https://limitlesstcg.com/cards/jp/SV8/33))
- Limitless credits Scarlet & Violet–onward card text/images to malie.io, older material to PTCGO/pokemon.com, and Japanese text/images to pokemon-card.com. It says it is unaffiliated with Pokémon. Thus the site is a curated, unofficial aggregation, not a first-party rules-name authority. ([About, “Credits”](https://limitlesstcg.com/about))
- Search supports `lang:jp` and `lang:en.t` (translated English for Japanese cards). Its [translation page](https://limitlesstcg.com/translations) shows both fully translated recent set entries and partially/untranslated older entries (e.g. SMD 2/19, CP5 0/1). These are set-level “new cards” figures, **not** a measured EN↔JA identity coverage percentage. Translated unreleased cards may lack an official English printed name. ([Search syntax](https://limitlesstcg.com/cards/syntax); [Translations](https://limitlesstcg.com/translations))
- The site exposes current format status and decklists on card pages, but those fields are time-varying. No inspected card-catalog versioning or update contract would make them safe to import as an auditable legality feed. ([SSP #57 page](https://limitlesstcg.com/cards/SSP/57))

## Access and rights

The published [developer guide](https://docs.limitlesstcg.com/developer.html) explicitly describes **tournament platform data**—placings, decklists, matches, games, and webhooks. Its [Games endpoints](https://docs.limitlesstcg.com/developer/games) supply game metadata and deck *categorization* rules, not a card catalog. The guide gives rate-limit headers and a higher-limit API-key request path for approved public-facing projects; those statements concern the documented tournament API. In the inspected owner-published docs, no card-catalog endpoint, bulk dataset, catalog rate/SLA contract, or automated card-page ingestion instructions were found. This negative finding is limited to the published surfaces inspected, not proof that no private feed exists.

The public [`robots.txt`](https://limitlesstcg.com/robots.txt), fetched on 2026-09-28, contained `User-agent: *` and an empty `Disallow:`. That is not a card-data reuse license, a supported integration contract, or a promise that automated extraction is acceptable at any volume. The [legal notice](https://limitlesstcg.com/legal) identifies the operator but offers no bulk-data grant; the site's footer attributes card images/text copyright to Pokémon and related rightsholders. Do not infer rights to copy or redistribute images/text from public visibility or from robots. If this source becomes necessary, contact Limitless for a published feed/API, permitted use, attribution, and operational limits, and separately review underlying content rights. ([About/footer](https://limitlesstcg.com/about); [Legal notice](https://limitlesstcg.com/legal))

## Recommendation and failure modes

1. **English MVP:** Keep TCGdex as the Printing catalog and derive an application-owned Card Identity from the exact normalized English card name. Group all same-name Printings, including mechanically different cards like the two `Pikachu ex` examples; do not group on Limitless's print relation, image, species, attacks, or collector number. Preserve the printed display name, distinguish meaningful suffixes (`ex`/`EX`, owner or regional qualifiers), and test category-specific and card-specific copy-limit exceptions separately. ([TCGdex Card schema](https://tcgdex.dev/reference/card); [official rules](https://www.pokemon-card.com/assets/document/manual.pdf))
2. **Optional quality assurance:** A human can use Limitless print pages to investigate suspected missing reprints, art variants, or source-name errors. Record the inspected URL and date and verify before changing local data. Do not make its website scrape part of startup, sync, or deck validation.
3. **Later mixed-language support:** A same-card English↔Japanese print link can propose a reviewed *name* pair, but the broader group must still include every Printing with either rules-equivalent name, even cards with different mechanics. Missing translations, unreleased translated glosses, stale pages, ambiguous names, and differing catalog coverage should produce an unresolved/review state rather than a silent merge or a falsely valid deck. See the [cross-language source audit](cross-language-card-identity-source.md).
4. **Reconsider only with new evidence:** A documented, licensed, stable Limitless card feed with IDs, coverage, change behavior, and operating limits could justify a bounded integration. Until then, it adds no essential Card Identity information to the English-only MVP beyond what exact TCGdex names provide.
